# Guyana Student Career Pathways Guide
*A multi-bucket guide from Form 1 through Form 6, CSEC/CVQ choices, CAPE planning and current post-secondary routes*

**Edition:** 1.0  
**Last verified:** 9 August 2026  
**Coverage:** 145 career-family profiles across 11 overlapping buckets

---

## HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE

Careers do not have to fit into only one category. Every profile can appear under several of these buckets: **Science & Medicine; Trade & TVET; Education; Sports; Engineering; Creative Industry; Technology; Business & Entrepreneurship; Public Service & Government; Law & Humanities; and Agriculture.**

The guide keeps one full profile for each career family and cross-lists it in every relevant bucket. This prevents conflicting copies while allowing a student to discover careers from different interests.

Three labels must not be confused:

- **Recommended CSEC/CAPE subjects** help a student prepare and preserve options.
- **Formal entry requirements** belong to a named institution, programme and intake.
- **Professional gates** include registration, licensing, supervised practice, fitness, appointment or competitive recruitment after education.

Profile status marks describe the route, not a promise of admission or employment:

- ✅ a current official or first-party pathway was confirmed;
- 🟡 the route exists but varies, is incomplete locally or depends on the intake/employer; and
- ⚠️ portfolio, talent, appointment or election matters more than a single credential.

No subject choice made in Form 1–3 should be presented as an irreversible lifetime decision. Students should preserve core skills, verify prerequisites before Form 4 and maintain a bridging route.

# PART 1 — THE FORM 1–6 ROADMAP

In Guyana, Forms 1–5 normally lead to CSEC, CVQ/SCCP or another secondary-leaving pathway. “Form 6” commonly includes **Lower Sixth/Grade 12** and **Upper Sixth/Grade 13**, where CAPE is usually completed across Unit 1 and Unit 2. Not every student needs CAPE: technical institute, CVQ, BIT, GSA, health-training, CPCE and employment-linked routes can begin after CSEC or another accepted credential.

## Form 1 — Build the base and explore broadly

- Protect **English, Mathematics, Science and digital literacy**. Every bucket uses them, even when a later programme does not formally require all four.
- Explore all 11 buckets through clubs, reading, career videos, safe practical projects and conversations with verified practitioners.
- Start a simple career notebook: interests, activities tried, skills enjoyed, subjects that need support and questions to research.
- Do not choose a single lifetime career. The Form 1 goal is exposure and foundation.

## Form 2 — Test interests through evidence

- Complete at least one small project in three different buckets—for example a science investigation, a business budget and a creative or technical product.
- Practise communication, teamwork, punctuality, safe internet use and personal organisation.
- Ask what the student likes doing repeatedly, not only what sounds prestigious.
- Begin a portfolio folder containing certificates, projects, reflections, results and community activities.

## Form 3 — Choose CSEC/CVQ subjects without closing key doors

- Check the **actual subjects offered by the school**; students cannot select from the whole CXC catalogue if the school does not teach them.
- Keep English A and Mathematics whenever possible. Then protect the prerequisite subjects needed for the student's strongest two or three career clusters.
- Compare at least three future routes: a degree route, a technical/vocational route and an alternative/bridging route.
- Where available, consider CVQ/SCCP alongside academic subjects. Current Ministry policy treats academic and competency-based pathways as parallel, not inferior.
- Obtain the current programme page before final subject selection; “science stream” or “business stream” is advice, not a substitute for a named admission rule.

## Form 4 — Build evidence, not only examination plans

- Begin CSEC/CVQ work seriously and keep SBA/project evidence organised according to current school and CXC rules.
- Join one sustained activity connected to a likely career: lab/science club, sport, debate, coding, agriculture, cadets, business, art, music, service or TVET.
- Learn to write a one-page student CV and request supervised workplace visits or job-shadowing where permitted.
- Review progress each term. A weak prerequisite subject needs early support, not panic in Form 5.

## Form 5 — Complete secondary credentials and apply on time

- Sit the appropriate CSEC/CVQ examinations and maintain the portfolio.
- Apply to suitable Form 6, UG, CPCE, technical institutes, GSA, Ministry training or apprenticeship routes according to their published calendars; some institutions accept applications while results are pending.
- Compare the formal minimum, competitive reality, location, equipment, transport, accommodation, licensing and any service obligation.
- Keep a Plan A, Plan B and bridging route. One disappointing grade should trigger a revised pathway, not abandonment of the career goal.

## Form 6 — Specialise and transition

### Lower Sixth / Grade 12

- Choose CAPE subjects by checking the intended programme, not by copying a friend's combination.
- Complete Unit 1 work, Communication Studies/Caribbean Studies or the school's required programme, and build research, writing and time-management skills.
- Shortlist tertiary programmes, regulators, scholarships and regional alternatives. Record deadlines and documents.

### Upper Sixth / Grade 13

- Complete relevant Unit 2 subjects and any programme requirements.
- Apply early, request transcripts/results, prepare references and verify every condition directly with the institution.
- For regulated careers, map the full chain: education → internship/experience → examination → registration/licence → first job.
- Students not taking CAPE can use this same period for CVQ progression, technical institute, BIT, health training, work attachment, a portfolio or supervised employment.

## The six-year student record

By the end of secondary school, every student should leave with: verified results; a short CV; a portfolio or practical evidence; at least two references; a list of programmes and deadlines; a primary route and backup route; and basic understanding of costs, region, transport, accommodation and professional licensing.

# PART 2 — CSEC, CAPE, CVQ AND ADMISSION RULES

## What CSEC grades mean

CXC reports CSEC Grades I–VI. CXC advises that Grades I–III at General or Technical Proficiency satisfy the usual matriculation benchmark for many tertiary routes, but each institution and programme may demand particular subjects, grades, sittings, ages or experience. Source: [CXC CSEC overview](https://www.cxc.org/examinations/csec-edit/).

In Guyana, the Ministry has also described secondary matriculation as either five CSEC subjects including English and Mathematics **or** a Level 1 CVQ certification in its school-TVET policy context. That statement does not override a university, regulator or employer's programme-specific rule. Source: [Ministry statement on vocational education](https://education.gov.gy/en/index.php/media2/news-events/7312-vocational-education-deserves-equal-respect).

## What CAPE does—and does not do

CAPE is an advanced post-secondary qualification normally taken during sixth form. Subjects are organised in Units; the exact units and grades required depend on the target programme. CAPE is useful for competitive science, medicine, law and overseas routes, but it is not automatically required for every UG degree or technical career. Sources: [CXC current syllabuses](https://www.cxc.org/syllabus-downloads/), [UG general admission requirements](https://registry.uog.edu.gy/admissions-division/general-admission-requirements).

## CXC assessment change students must know

In July 2026, CXC announced a transition for **non-practical subjects** from traditional school-based assessment toward Paper 032: CAPE candidates transition in May–June 2027; CSEC schools may choose SBA or Paper 032 in 2027, with full CSEC transition planned for May–June 2028. Students must follow their school's current instructions because implementation depends on examination year and subject type. Source: [CXC SBA reform announcement](https://www.cxc.org/holding-to-the-standard/).

## Eleven-bucket subject-planning matrix

Careers may belong to several buckets. A student should use the **union of the essential prerequisites**, then choose a realistic subject load with the school counsellor.

<a id="bucket-science-medicine"></a>

## Science & Medicine

**Forms 1–3 exploration:** Use science clubs, health fairs, first-aid activities, laboratory demonstrations and careful observation of health and environmental work. Protect strong English and Mathematics foundations.

**Recommended CSEC planning:** English A, Mathematics, Biology, Chemistry, Physics or Integrated Science; Human and Social Biology is useful for many care routes but is not accepted as a substitute for Biology by every programme.

**Recommended CAPE planning:** Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Pure or Applied Mathematics, Environmental Science, Food and Nutrition and Communication Studies, depending on the intended programme.

**After CSEC/Form 6:** UG science/health programmes, Ministry of Health training, approved regional programmes, supervised clinical training and professional registration where required.

<a id="bucket-trade-tvet"></a>

## Trade & TVET

**Forms 1–3 exploration:** Try practical projects safely, learn measurement and tool care, practise numeracy, join school TVET activities and observe qualified workers. Never substitute unsafe informal work for supervised training.

**Recommended CSEC planning:** English A and Mathematics plus the most relevant technical subject: Industrial Technology, Technical Drawing, Information Technology, EDPM, Agricultural Science, Home Economics, Food and Nutrition or an available CVQ/SCCP occupational area.

**Recommended CAPE planning:** CAPE is not required for many direct TVET routes. Useful options include Building and Mechanical Engineering, Electrical and Electronic Engineering Technology, Design and Technology, Green Engineering, Entrepreneurship and Management of Business.

**After CSEC/Form 6:** School CVQ/SCCP, BIT, practical instruction centres, technical institutes, Carnegie, GSA, apprenticeship, employer training and competency/licensing routes.

<a id="bucket-education"></a>

## Education

**Forms 1–3 exploration:** Tutor peers, read widely, practise presentations, volunteer with supervised youth activities and note whether the student enjoys explaining, planning and supporting learning.

**Recommended CSEC planning:** English A, Mathematics, Information Technology and strong passes in the subject areas the student may later teach. Social Studies, Human and Social Biology and a modern language can be useful.

**Recommended CAPE planning:** Communication Studies, Caribbean Studies and the academic subjects to be taught; Sociology, Literatures in English, History, Mathematics, sciences and Information Technology may support specialisations.

**After CSEC/Form 6:** CPCE teacher education, UG education/humanities routes, specialist training and supervised teaching or education-support roles.

<a id="bucket-sports"></a>

## Sports

**Forms 1–3 exploration:** Play more than one sport, learn rules and injury prevention, maintain academics, record verified results and develop teamwork rather than specialising too early.

**Recommended CSEC planning:** English A, Mathematics, Physical Education and Sport, Biology or Human and Social Biology, Information Technology and Principles of Business.

**Recommended CAPE planning:** Physical Education and Sport, Biology, Communication Studies, Caribbean Studies, Entrepreneurship or Management of Business.

**After CSEC/Form 6:** Club and federation pathways, coaching/officiating credentials, sport administration, tertiary study and health-science routes for sports medicine and rehabilitation.

<a id="bucket-engineering"></a>

## Engineering

**Forms 1–3 exploration:** Build and test small projects, practise measurement and drawing, strengthen algebra and science, learn safe workshop behaviour and join robotics, CAD or problem-solving activities where available.

**Recommended CSEC planning:** English A, Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry or Integrated Science, Technical Drawing, Information Technology and Additional Mathematics where available.

**Recommended CAPE planning:** Pure and/or Applied Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Building and Mechanical Engineering, Electrical and Electronic Engineering Technology, Green Engineering, Design and Technology or Computer Science.

**After CSEC/Form 6:** UG engineering/built-environment routes, technical institutes, GTEE/CVQ pathways, approved aviation/maritime training and profession-specific experience or registration.

<a id="bucket-creative-industry"></a>

## Creative Industry

**Forms 1–3 exploration:** Make original work, keep dated drafts, learn copyright and consent, perform or exhibit safely, practise communication and begin a portfolio without abandoning core academics.

**Recommended CSEC planning:** English A, Visual Arts, Music, Theatre Arts, Information Technology, EDPM and Principles of Business, selected according to the medium.

**Recommended CAPE planning:** Art and Design, Digital Media, Animation and Game Design, Performing Arts, Literatures in English, Communication Studies, Entrepreneurship or Information Technology.

**After CSEC/Form 6:** Portfolio-based work, apprenticeships, CVQ/TVET production routes, UG communication/technology studies, specialist regional study and entrepreneurship.

<a id="bucket-technology"></a>

## Technology

**Forms 1–3 exploration:** Learn safe and responsible computing, build small programs or digital projects, understand privacy and security, practise logical thinking and document work in a portfolio.

**Recommended CSEC planning:** English A, Mathematics, Information Technology, Physics, EDPM and Additional Mathematics where available.

**Recommended CAPE planning:** Computer Science, Information Technology, Pure or Applied Mathematics, Physics, Digital Media, Animation and Game Design or Electrical and Electronic Engineering Technology.

**After CSEC/Form 6:** UG computer-science/IT/information-systems routes, technical institutes, vendor training, portfolios, internships and progressively responsible technical work.

<a id="bucket-business-entrepreneurship"></a>

## Business & Entrepreneurship

**Forms 1–3 exploration:** Practise budgeting, spreadsheets, selling ethically, teamwork, record-keeping and public speaking. Run small supervised projects rather than taking financial risks.

**Recommended CSEC planning:** English A, Mathematics, Principles of Accounts, Principles of Business, Economics, EDPM, Office Administration and Information Technology.

**Recommended CAPE planning:** Accounting, Economics, Management of Business, Entrepreneurship, Financial Services Studies, Logistics and Supply Chain Operations, Law, Information Technology and Communication Studies.

**After CSEC/Form 6:** UG SEBI and social-science routes, technical/business diplomas, employer apprenticeships, professional qualifications and responsible entrepreneurship.

<a id="bucket-public-service-government"></a>

## Public Service & Government

**Forms 1–3 exploration:** Join service, debate, student leadership, cadet or community activities; build fitness where relevant; learn civics, ethics, teamwork and accurate record-keeping.

**Recommended CSEC planning:** English A, Mathematics, Social Studies, Caribbean History, Geography, Information Technology, Physical Education and a modern language, depending on the role.

**Recommended CAPE planning:** Communication Studies, Caribbean Studies, Law, Sociology, History, Geography, Economics, Information Technology and relevant sciences.

**After CSEC/Form 6:** Vacancy-specific government recruitment, UG/CPCE/technical qualifications, uniformed-service selection and training, or public-policy and community-development routes.

<a id="bucket-law-humanities"></a>

## Law & Humanities

**Forms 1–3 exploration:** Read widely, write evidence-based arguments, debate respectfully, learn source checking and observe civic, cultural and community institutions.

**Recommended CSEC planning:** English A, English B, Caribbean History, Social Studies, Geography, Religious Education, Economics and a modern language. Mathematics remains valuable even where it is not a formal entry requirement.

**Recommended CAPE planning:** Law, Communication Studies, Caribbean Studies, History, Sociology, Literatures in English, Economics, Geography and modern languages.

**After CSEC/Form 6:** UG law, social-science and humanities routes, regional professional education, public-service recruitment, research, communication and community work.

<a id="bucket-agriculture"></a>

## Agriculture

**Forms 1–3 exploration:** Grow or monitor a small supervised project, record inputs and results, learn soil/water/animal welfare basics, visit farms or agroprocessors and treat agriculture as science plus business.

**Recommended CSEC planning:** English A, Mathematics, Agricultural Science, Biology, Chemistry or Integrated Science, Geography, Food and Nutrition and Principles of Business.

**Recommended CAPE planning:** Agricultural Science, Biology, Chemistry, Environmental Science, Geography, Food and Nutrition, Entrepreneurship, Green Engineering and Communication Studies.

**After CSEC/Form 6:** GSA, UG agriculture/forestry/science routes, school CVQ, BIT/TVET, farm or fisheries training, research/extension work and agribusiness.

# PART 3 — CURRENT GUYANA EDUCATION AND TRAINING LANDSCAPE

- The official TVET Hub currently lists **155 programmes** from more than 20 institutions. Availability, dates and credentials differ by provider; use the live filters. [TVET Hub](https://tvet.gov.gy/programs)
- Guyana's National TVET Policy 2025–2035 is intended to align vocational education with oil and gas, climate-resilient agriculture, low-carbon technology and digital development. [National TVET Policy announcement](https://education.gov.gy/en/index.php/media2/news-events/8158-press-release-national-tvet-policy-2025-2035-unveiled-to-strengthen-workforce-development)
- UG states that approved Guyanese students attend under the Government's free-tuition policy from 2025. Admission remains competitive and programme-specific. [UG registration procedure](https://registry.uog.edu.gy/admissions-division/procedure-registration)
- GSA's 2026 allocation covers the removal of tuition fees, but students should still verify application, accommodation, equipment and other possible costs. [GSA tuition update](https://agriculture.gov.gy/2026/02/12/gsa-receives-increased-funding-to-subsidise-tuition-fees-removal/)
- The Ministry of Labour's BIT provides skills training by intake and region; course lists and conditions change. [BIT updates](https://labour.gov.gy/category/board-of-industrial-training-bit/)
- Scholarships and application windows are dated opportunities, not permanent rights. Check the current [Government scholarships page](https://mps.gov.gy/scholarships/gog/) and the institution before applying.

This guide does not promise admission, funding, licensing or employment. It is a planning map that points students to the controlling source.

**Buckets:** [Business & Entrepreneurship](#bucket-business-entrepreneurship)

**Current routes:** BSc Accountancy or related SEBI route, followed where desired by ACCA or another recognised professional qualification. Sources: [UG BSc Accountancy](https://sebi.uog.edu.gy/srms/departments/246/programmes/865/details), [ICAG membership](https://icag.org.gy/membership/membership-application/).  
**Current development:** UG's accountancy programme received ACCA accreditation for 2026–2030, with four exemptions reported for qualifying graduates from 2026. Confirm the exemption calculator against the student's graduation year. Source: [UG/ACCA accreditation report](https://newsroom.gy/2026/06/23/ug-now-accredited-to-offer-acca-certification/).  
**First jobs:** accounts assistant, audit associate, tax assistant, finance officer and internal-audit trainee.

**Form 6 — recommended CAPE:** Biology and Chemistry are the safest science pair for many health routes; add Physics or Mathematics according to the target programme.  

No current local veterinary degree was confirmed. Veterinary medicine is currently a regional/international study pathway unless a new Guyanese degree or formal scholarship arrangement is identified. Guyana School of Agriculture certificates and diplomas in animal or livestock production support agricultural work but do not qualify someone as a veterinarian.

## Visual Artist / Photographer / Craft Artist ⚠️

**Buckets:** [Creative Industry](#bucket-creative-industry) · [Business & Entrepreneurship](#bucket-business-entrepreneurship)  
**Forms 1–3:** Create original work, keep dated drafts, learn consent and copyright, and build a portfolio while maintaining core academics.  
**Forms 4–5 — recommended CSEC:** English A plus the relevant creative subjects: Visual Arts, Music, Theatre Arts, Information Technology, EDPM and Principles of Business.  
**Form 6 — recommended CAPE:** Art and Design, Digital Media, Animation and Game Design, Performing Arts, Literatures in English, Communication Studies or Entrepreneurship.  

E.R. Burrowes School of Art and UG Fine Arts are relevant formal routes, but portfolio development, exhibitions, commissions and entrepreneurship are central. Earlier claims that every Burrowes route is free or accepts applicants with no qualification were not verified for the current intake and should not be hard-coded.

## Welder / Fabricator / Metalworker ✅

**Buckets:** [Trade & TVET](#bucket-trade-tvet) · [Engineering](#bucket-engineering)  
**Forms 1–3:** Develop numeracy, measurement, drawing, tool safety and supervised practical problem-solving; keep evidence of completed projects.  
**Forms 4–5 — recommended CSEC:** English A, Mathematics and the most relevant Industrial Technology, Technical Drawing, Information Technology, science or available CVQ/SCCP occupational subject.  
**Form 6 — recommended CAPE:** CAPE is optional for many direct trade routes; relevant engineering, design, technology, business or entrepreneurship subjects can support later progression.  

**Helpful subjects:** Mathematics, Technical Drawing, Mechanical/Metalwork Technology, Physics and English.  
**Routes:** CVQ/technical-institute Welding or Metal Work Engineering, BIT and employer apprenticeship. Source: [GITC programmes](https://tvet.gov.gy/programs?institution=72).  
**First jobs:** welding assistant, fabricator, metalworker and structural-welding trainee. Processes, positions, coded tests and inspection requirements must be stored separately.

**First roles:** editorial assistant, communications assistant, copywriter, reporter trainee, proofreader and independent creator.

# PART 6 — APPLICATION AND VERIFICATION CHECKLIST

Before a student commits to a route, record:

1. Exact programme and institution.
2. Intake year, deadline, campus/region and delivery mode.
3. Required CSEC subjects, grades and number of sittings.
4. Required CAPE subjects/Units, if any.
5. Whether CVQ, SCCP, GTEE, diploma, portfolio, experience or an entrance examination is accepted.
6. Age, medical, fitness, police-clearance, citizenship or interview requirements.
7. Tuition policy plus application, examination, equipment, transport, accommodation and licensing costs.
8. Internship, work attachment, service obligation, professional examination and regulator.
9. First realistic job—not only the eventual senior title.
10. A backup/bridging route and the date the information was verified.
